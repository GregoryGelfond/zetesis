The zetesis Book

CPU comparison for the 0.8.0 candidate

On 15 November 2026, the qualified 0.8.0 candidate was compared with clingo across 315 distinct workloads. Of 299 complete paired timings, the established 3% band gives 214 zetesis wins, nine close and 76 clingo wins. The other 16 retain an unavailable reference; they are not timing wins. Strictly comparing the recorded times gives 214 zetesis-faster and 85 clingo-faster observations, with no exact equality. All nine close cases slightly favor clingo.

The per-case TSV gives every selected workload and exact nanosecond times. The receipt records SHA-256 workload identities, source files and amendments, contracts, profiles, report hashes, duplicate observations and classification. The full table below is the same 315-workload population. Historical 0.7.0 measurements remain unchanged.

Conditions and interpretation

The measured source was qualified as 3d395e57f14ca39c8ab436dfc1dbb7439982e4b7, then incorporated without a source-tree change into dev/0.8.0 at aa0df11fbd76420e557a858c053d29581a19e1d6. The runs preceded the release-number and release-documentation changes; the frozen executable still identified itself as 0.7.0. These are retained measurements of the implementation, not a fresh repeat after the merge. Capture ran from 06:30:10 to 07:10:36 UTC on 15 November 2026.

Zetesis used 14 CPU workers on an Apple M4 Pro, automatic grounding and checking, and a 24 GiB named-storage allowance. Clingo 5.8.2 used one worker. Each solver had one qualification run and one timed fresh process, no warmup and no separate RSS run. The outer process deadline was 120 seconds, except for the reported-planner panel, which allowed 130 seconds around the solver’s cooperative 120-second deadline; the scalability panel requested indexed formula joins. The receipt preserves these per-panel controls. All native execution was on CPU; these results do not measure GPU speed.

Elapsed time includes fresh-process startup, grounding, solving and captured JSON output, with statistics enabled for zetesis. It excludes comparison hashing. Qualification requires complete selected answer families, including multiplicity, final optimal ties and costs. Native full models also agree between qualification and timing; hidden reference interpretations are not observed. Interrupted or reference-unavailable runs do not receive a timing vote.

The exact zetesis/clingo ratio is classified below 0.97 as a zetesis win and above 1.03 as a clingo win; both boundaries count as close. This is the published 3% reporting convention, not a statistical equivalence test. One sample is a performance screen: small differences and parallel-search variation need repeated measurement before a causal improvement is claimed. The canonical benchmark’s strict faster/slower verdicts remain in the receipt.

The original schedule has 320 positions. Five extra positions repeat three workload identities: task allocation and SEND+MORE=MONEY occur in three panels each, and queens variant 02 in two. Each gets one vote in its original corpus position, using the SHA-256 identity rather than its display label. All repeated observations have the same faster/slower and 3% classification. No timing samples are pooled across panels and no summed process total is presented as one elapsed corpus run.

On the 16 unavailable arithmetic references, clingo reached the qualification deadline. Zetesis completed with exhaustive UNSAT and no interruption on each; clingo supplied no confirming complete family. Paired timing was not attempted. The 0.7.0 record used a 600-second deadline, three timed samples and 299 inputs, so neither its 13 incomplete cases nor its headline win count is directly comparable with this campaign.

Reproducing the selections

Use zetesis-bench; no separate timing or model-decoding implementation is needed. From the repository root, build the solver and benchmark tool with the pinned toolchain, then run a canonical panel:

cargo build --locked --release --all-features -p zetesis-cli -p zetesis-bench
ROOT=$(pwd -P)
NATIVE="$ROOT/target/release/zetesis"
BENCH="$ROOT/target/release/zetesis-bench"
CLINGO=$(command -v clingo)
OUT=$(mktemp -d)

"$BENCH" run "$ROOT/examples/correctness" --suite corpus \
  --zetesis "$NATIVE" --clingo "$CLINGO" --native-interface solve \
  --backend cpu --grounder auto --oracle auto --threads 14 --clingo-threads 1 \
  --memory 24GiB --warmups 0 --repetitions 1 --memory-runs 0 \
  --timeout-seconds 120 --campaign-seconds 7200 \
  --sample-bytes 134217728 --answer-bytes 134217728 \
  --native-report-bytes 134217728 --native-atoms 4194304 \
  --native-value-nodes 8388608 --capture-bytes 2147483648 \
  --report-bytes 4294967296 --report "$OUT/corpus95.json" --json --color never

These commands reproduce the selection and protocol, not the timing of a particular machine. Compare an old and new saved report with zetesis-bench compare before=before.json after=after.json; the tool requires matching identities and profiles. Retain the supplied limits and all failed slots. A fresh comparison should use enough repeated samples for the claim it is intended to support.
