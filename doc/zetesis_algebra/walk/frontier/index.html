Module frontier 

Source
Expand description

Shared work-stealing frontier and termination protocols.

A frontier is one deque of regions per worker and the termination record that carries the count of unresolved regions and the gate idle workers wait at. The CPU walker and the GPU-route producers share every protocol here: taking a worker’s own newest region, stealing a peer’s oldest, publishing a split’s children, and the idle back-off that waits for work or the end of the walk.

Each deque has its own mutex: local removal locks only that deque, a split publication locks that deque and then, after releasing it and only while a worker is idle, the idle gate to wake one, and a thief skips a busy peer. Region preparation and evaluation hold no deque lock. Entry capacity grows fallibly and before any child is published. An atomic counter of unresolved regions carries termination: a split raises it before pushing its children, a resolved region lowers it once, and an unsuccessful thief stops only once that counter has reached zero. An atomic closed flag, set on the first stop or when the walk’s consumer stops listening, halts the others at their next region. Idle workers wait at a gate that the last resolution and every close open, and that a publication opens for one waiter, so none sleeps out its timed wait after the walk has ended or while a region it could take is published. A publication reads the number of idle workers without the gate and takes the gate only when one is counted, so busy workers never contend for it. The timed wait bounds how late an idle worker sees a cancellation, and how late it finds a region left in a deque that was busy when it last looked.
