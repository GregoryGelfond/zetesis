Expand description

What ends the walk — every region resolved, or a close — and the gate idle workers wait at for either.

The count and the flag are atomics because the region loop reads them for every region. Every transition that ends the walk changes its atomic first and then takes the gate to wake the waiters, and an idle worker takes the gate and re-checks both conditions, and the peers’ deques, before it waits. Whichever reaches the gate first, no idle worker waits past the end of the walk: a worker that takes the gate after the ending transition sees it, and one that took the gate before is already waiting when the wake comes.

The number of idle waiters is an atomic changed only while the gate is held, and a publication reads it without the gate. A waiter registers itself, issues a sequentially consistent fence, and only then re-checks the deques; a publication pushes its children, releases its deque, issues a sequentially consistent fence, and only then reads the count, taking the gate to wake one waiter when it is not zero. The two fences are totally ordered. If the publication’s comes first, the waiter’s re-check observes the released deque and so the pushed child; if the waiter’s comes first, the publication reads its registration and takes the gate, which the waiter holds until it is waiting, so the wake reaches it. Either the waiter sees the child or the publication sees the waiter. Zero is terminal, since a region in hand counts until it is resolved. Every wait stays bounded by its timeout, so correctness never rests on a wake: the timeout is how an idle worker sees a cancellation, or a region left in a deque its re-check found busy.

The first case rests on a premise about the platform’s mutex: releasing it and try_lock are atomic operations on its lock word, as they are in std’s own futex mutex (Linux, Windows) and in the pthread mutex std uses on macOS, so the fences order them as they order any atomic access. A re-check after the publication’s fence therefore finds the deque released, or locked again by a later holder, which costs the waiter a bounded wait. Were the premise to fail, a waiter could also find the publication still holding the deque, and would find the child after its bounded wait.

Fields§

§gate: Mutex<()>

Held to register and re-check before an idle wait, to wake one waiter after a publication that counted one, and to wake every waiter after the walk ends.

§idle_waiters: AtomicUsize

The number of idle workers waiting on idle: changed only while gate is held, read by a publication without it.
